Traveler and family rights

Nationality is legally relevant; usual residence is context, not automatically the exemption test.

Private profile
Family-rights rule: Accompanied/joining periods in an EU host country other than the EU citizen's state of nationality can qualify for the EU free-movement regime. They do not automatically count toward the ordinary Schengen-wide 90/180 ceiling. The EU citizen's own country normally requires national-law analysis. Family C-visa wording alone does not prove that any particular day is exempt. Other EEA/Swiss host situations are flagged for separate review.

Visa and immigration document

A short-stay visa sticker, residence status and free-movement rights are separate questions. Enter sticker details when available.

For MULT visas, the sticker duration is generally a stay ceiling within the relevant short-stay conditions. For single/double-entry visas, authorized days are often a visa-total limit; enter days already used to assess what remains.

Important: For a qualifying EU family-member journey, substantive entry/residence rights must not be reduced to a simplistic visa-expiry test. Conversely, an unexpired family visa does not by itself exempt solo stays or visits to the EU citizen's own country. This application's visa check reports the ordinary sticker scenario separately.
